# Pakistan: The Journey — design direction

## Three possible directions

### Theme Name: Museum Film
Very Brief Intro: A cinematic digital museum with archival paper, film grain, long vertical pauses, and a palette that develops from monochrome memory into living color. The visitor moves through history as if turning the reels of a national documentary.
Probability: 0.06

### Theme Name: Atlas of Becoming
Very Brief Intro: A cartographic narrative where each chapter unfolds through maps, routes, coordinates, and shifting topographic lines. The emotional tone is thoughtful and geographic, with the country’s shape acting as the recurring visual anchor.
Probability: 0.04

### Theme Name: Future Archive
Very Brief Intro: A speculative archive that pairs historic documents with luminous data traces, youth voices, and a forward-looking 2026 interface. It would feel more digital and experimental than the other directions.
Probability: 0.08

## Chosen approach: Museum Film

### Design Movement
Editorial documentary design blended with museum exhibition graphics, archival film titles, restrained Swiss typography, and South Asian material references.

### Core Principles
1. **Time has a temperature:** early chapters use charcoal, ivory, and sepia; later chapters gradually earn emerald, cobalt, saffron, and electric cyan.
2. **Silence creates gravity:** large pauses, short captions, and generous negative space let difficult moments land without spectacle.
3. **Evidence before decoration:** dates, sources, and quote attributions are visible; decorative layers support the story rather than compete with it.
4. **The scroll is the camera:** the visitor moves from wide establishing scenes to intimate text fragments and back out to a collective horizon.

### Color Philosophy
The page begins almost uncolored, like a contact sheet from an archive. Warm sepia enters during the movement and birth chapters, deepens into restrained national green during nation-building, then expands into vivid contemporary color. The final palette is bright but not glossy: hope is presented as earned light.

### Layout Paradigm
A vertical sequence of full-bleed chapter stages connected by a fixed side timeline. Each stage uses a different composition: split-screen archive, centered date monument, offset editorial notes, horizontal event strip, kinetic modern collage, and a final open horizon.

### Signature Elements
1. A fixed “film reel” progress rail with chapter numbers, years, and a thin moving playhead.
2. Monospace archival labels, image captions, source notes, and visible frame marks.
3. Parallax layers of paper, dust, map lines, photographs, and soft light leaks.

### Interaction Philosophy
Scroll reveals are quiet and intentional. Hovering or focusing a chapter marker previews its title; clicking a marker scrolls to it. Reduced motion keeps the complete narrative readable without animation.

### Animation
Use opacity and transform for chapter entrances, very slow parallax for background materials, gentle film-grain drift, and one restrained color transition between eras. No constant motion on text. All non-essential animation is disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Typography System
Display: **Cormorant Garamond** for historical titles and emotional statements. Interface/body: **DM Sans** for clarity. Metadata: **IBM Plex Mono** for dates, citations, and museum labels.

### Brand Essence
An immersive digital museum of Pakistan’s making and becoming, for anyone who wants to feel the timeline rather than memorize it. Personality: **reflective, cinematic, hopeful**.

### Brand Voice
Captions are short and composed; headings are human but not sentimental; calls to action invite reflection rather than demand conversion.

Example lines: “Every nation begins as an idea held in common.” / “The story continues with us.”

### Wordmark & Logo
Use a compact vertical wordmark: “PAKISTAN” in spaced uppercase with “THE JOURNEY” beneath in a smaller monospace line, paired with a simple frame-like crescent mark.

### Signature Brand Color
**Archive Emerald — #0D6B52**: introduced sparingly in the early chapters, then fully inhabited in the modern and future chapters.

## Content and source policy

Historical claims are written from verified government, UNDP, World Bank, and U.S. Census Bureau references saved in `research-notes.md`. The site should show a concise “Sources & context” note at the end rather than pretending the story is exhaustive.
